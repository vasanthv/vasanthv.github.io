---
title: "Stupidity can look like bravery"
date: 2026-11-13
tags: [thoughts]
---

Imagine a brave person.

What comes to your mind?

We often picture someone fighting for their nation, someone willing to sacrifice their own life to protect others. We admire such people for their courage, their determination, and their willingness to put others before themselves.

But what is true bravery?

To me, bravery is doing what needs to be done even when you are scared to do it.

Imagine witnessing someone being bullied. You know that standing up for them might put you in trouble. The bully might turn against you. You might become the next target. You are aware of the risks, and naturally, you are afraid.

But despite that fear, you choose to stand up for the person being bullied.

That, to me, is bravery.

You recognise the danger, you understand the possible consequences, but you decide that doing what is right is more important than staying back.

However, there is another kind of action that can look exactly like bravery from the outside. It is not courage, though. It is stupidity disguised as bravery.

Let's take the same example.

Someone is being bullied, and another person steps in to defend them. From the outside, both people might appear equally brave. Both confront the bully. Both put themselves in a potentially difficult situation.

But look a little deeper.

What if the second person doesn't step in because they care about the victim? What if they do it simply to prove their superiority? To show everyone how fearless they are? To establish that nobody can challenge them?

They aren't necessarily being brave. They might simply be too foolish to consider what could happen next.

There is a **BIG** difference between knowing the risks and choosing to face them, and not even thinking about the risks in the first place.

This doesn't mean that a brave person must always calculate every possible outcome or carefully plan every action. Sometimes, doing the right thing requires acting immediately. But there is a difference between acting despite knowing the danger and acting because you haven't bothered to recognise it.

> True bravery is not about having no fear or ignoring danger; it is about facing your fear to do what is right.
